Know which animal you just photographed.

HerpetoID identifies and re-identifies individual amphibians and reptiles from the natural patterns they already carry — belly spots, dorsal blotches, head scales. No toe-clipping, no PIT tags, no permanent marks of any kind.

Mark–recapture without the marks

Amphibians and reptiles are awkward animals to mark. They are small, they shed, they are often strictly protected, and the classic methods — toe-clipping, branding, PIT tags — cost the animal something. Many of them, meanwhile, carry a pattern as individual as a fingerprint. HerpetoID turns a folder of field photographs into a searchable catalogue of known individuals.

◎

No permanent marking

The photograph is the tag. No toe-clipping, no branding, no PIT tag, no batch mark that costs you individual identity.

⚊

Less handling, less risk

Chytrid, Bsal, ranavirus and snake fungal disease travel on hands and equipment. Every minute of handling you can drop is worth dropping.

☑

You make the call

The software ranks candidate matches and shows the evidence. The herpetologist confirms or rejects — never the algorithm alone.

✈

Offline, and it stays that way

No account, no upload, no connection needed at the pond. Localities for persecuted and collected species never leave your machine.

What carries the pattern

Different groups wear their identity in different places. A species module tells HerpetoID where to look and how to read it.

Newts & salamanders

Ventral spot and blotch patterns; the dorsal yellow of Salamandra. The classic, best-established photo-ID group.

Frogs & toads

Dorsal markings, flank and thigh patterning — and in some species the iris.

Snakes

Head scale arrangement, dorsal zigzags and blotches, ventral scale markings.

Lizards

Dorsal and lateral spot rows, ocelli, throat and gular patterning.

Tortoises & terrapins

Carapace and plastron scute patterns, and head and neck markings.

…and whatever you study

None of these are built into the core. Each is a plugin — including the one you write.

From photo to identified individual

  1. Import your session

    Drop in the photographs from a survey night or a transect. Dates, locations and camera metadata are read automatically.

  2. Mark the pattern

    Outline the region that carries the markings — the belly plate, the head shields, the plastron. The species module knows how to normalise and clean it up.

  3. Search the catalogue

    The matching algorithm compares the pattern against every individual already recorded and returns a ranked shortlist.

  4. Confirm and record

    Review the candidates side by side. Confirm a recapture or register a new individual — then export for your survival and abundance models.

One platform, many species

HerpetoID has no species baked into it. Each taxon arrives as a plugin that declares its own region of interest, its own field measurements — snout–vent length, sex, life stage, whatever your protocol records — and its own validation rules. The application builds the forms, comparison panels, statistics and exports to match.

Species modules

Add a newt, a viper or a tortoise without touching the core. Announced here as they are released.

Matching algorithms

Classic keypoint matchers today, learned embeddings tomorrow — installed the same drop-in way, and shared across every species.

Open by design

MIT licensed, auditable, and documented so any herp lab can write its own module.
