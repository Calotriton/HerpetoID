Free & open source · Windows

Know which animal you just photographed.

HerpetoID identifies and re-identifies individual animals from the natural patterns on their bodies — no tags, no clipping, no handling beyond the photograph itself.

Mark–recapture without the marks

Many animals carry a biometric of their own: the ventral spots of a newt, the belly pattern of a toad, the flank markings of a salamander. HerpetoID turns a folder of field photographs into a searchable catalogue of known individuals.

◎

Non-invasive

The photograph is the tag. No toe-clipping, no PIT tags, no recapture stress.

☑

You make the call

The software ranks candidate matches and shows the evidence. The scientist confirms or rejects — never the algorithm alone.

✈

Offline first

Everything runs on your machine. No account, no upload, no connection needed in the field.

◫

Portable projects

A project is one self-contained folder — database, images and metadata. Copy it, back it up, hand it to a colleague.

From photo to identified individual

  1. Import your session

    Drop in the photographs from a survey. Dates, locations and camera metadata are read automatically.

  2. Mark the pattern

    Outline the region that carries the markings. The species module knows how to normalise and clean it up.

  3. Search the catalogue

    The matching algorithm compares the pattern against every individual already recorded and returns a ranked shortlist.

  4. Confirm and record

    Review the candidates side by side. Confirm a recapture or register a new individual — then export for your analysis.

One platform, many species

HerpetoID has no species baked into it. Each taxon arrives as a plugin that declares its own regions of interest, its own field measurements and its own validation rules — and the application builds the forms, comparison panels, statistics and exports to match.

Species modules

Add a new animal without touching the core. Announced here as they are released.

Matching algorithms

Classic keypoint matchers today, learned embeddings tomorrow — installed the same drop-in way.

Open by design

MIT licensed, auditable, and documented so any lab can write its own extension.
